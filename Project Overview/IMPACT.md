# Impact and organizer responsibilities

The project should help students explain how a question becomes a defensible decision: question → data → financial calculation and model → evaluation → uncertainty → decision brief.

## What Raymundo provides

| Organizer responsibility | What students receive | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose a manageable question | One shared challenge, a small dataset, clear success criteria | Approved challenge brief |
| Onboard and support all five students | Setup help, a first task, a peer reviewer, regular access to the lead | Five onboarding entries and a working task board |
| Prepare the same equipment | Two working Jetsons, six calculators, fair scheduled access | Setup tests, equipment bookings, condition notes |
| Connect the learning topics | Three guided sessions joining finance, data/modeling, and hardware | Lesson packets, student outputs, feedback |
| Give useful feedback | Specific review comments and help resolving blockers | Peer checks and Raymundo's final reviews |
| Arrange a real audience | A demonstration where participants can change an assumption and question the result | Demo record and decision brief |
| Preserve the work | Instructions the next cohort can follow | Reproduction check and artifact index |

Raymundo is the only day-to-day lead and final project approver. Summer summarizes reviewed progress for the funder. There is no required Sanchin/Cail approval in this alternate plan.

## Five contributors and wider participation

Five students have been accepted to build the project. The original 20–30-student reach figure remains an aspirational outreach target, subject to capacity and any applicable funding agreement. It is not a claim that 20–30 students joined the team. Changing this operating plan does not silently change a sponsor commitment.

Record core contributors, unique workshop attendees, and total attendance visits separately. A student attending three sessions is one unique participant and three attendance visits. Report overlap when stating an overall unique reach; do not add cohort and audience counts without accounting for it. Keep individual identities out of public attendance records.

## Minimum outcomes

- Five students complete onboarding and contribute linked evidence.
- Three guided learning experiences cover financial assumptions, data/model comparison, and Jetson/sensitivity work.
- One shared analysis has a transparent baseline, documented data, metrics, and limitations.
- One short decision brief states the recommendation or non-recommendation, assumptions, uncertainty, and what cannot be concluded.
- One demonstration lets an audience change an assumption and see why the result changes.
- A different operator can reproduce the package, and equipment is documented for reuse.

These are planned outcomes, not completed results. Use short before/after concept checks or anonymous feedback to assess learning alongside attendance. With only five contributors, report counts and concrete examples rather than claiming broad statistical evidence of impact.

## Demonstration format

Explain the question in five minutes. Show a calculator result, baseline-versus-model comparison, and documented Jetson run. Change a cost, demand, or discount-rate assumption. Explain what changed, what stayed uncertain, and what evidence would be needed next. Close with questions from the audience.

A public or simulated dataset is sufficient. The purpose is education and quantitative reasoning; no private financial records, real-money trading, or individualized investment advice are required.
